Auditor and reviewer guide

Try to break the claim.

A useful review does more than rerun a command. It identifies the maximum public claim, follows its evidence, tests the strongest alternative explanation, and records a disposition such as clean, wording fix, evidence gap, blocked, unresolved, invalid, uncheckable, or not claimed.

Review sequence

Start with scope, then attack the derivation.

  1. Name the maximum claim. Copy the exact sentence or claim ID. Record the legal work, address, date range, source profile, and strongest conclusion a reader could take from it.
  2. Bind the inspected state. Record the dated website, software, source or corpus snapshots, configuration, release status, and unavailable artifacts.
  3. Trace consequential statements. Follow each number, capability statement, and disposition to a public source, registry record, specification, implementation path, test, or external notice.
  4. Construct the strongest alternative. Ask whether source incompleteness, manifestation drift, parser error, temporal mismatch, editorial convention, stale comparison data, or a different authority model could explain the result.
  5. Check mechanism-to-wording fit. A hash supports identity; conservation supports an account over a declared universe; a mutation audit supports a footprint relation. None alone establishes legal meaning.
  6. Record the disposition. Use the protocol’s reviewer values: clean, wording fix, evidence gap, blocked, unresolved, invalid, uncheckable, or not claimed. If the public claim changes, map it separately to the claim registry’s bounded outcome vocabulary. Silence and lack of refutation are not confirmation.
  7. Change text and registry together. After adjudication, update the public claim, evidence record, limitations, and snapshot date or release binding.

Eight attack surfaces

Challenge the layer that actually carries the claim.

Eight assurance attack surfaces

SourceAre the exact artifacts identified, and is the declared horizon adequate?A valid hash proves a complete or legally correct source universe.

Missing artifacts

Stop where reconstruction would invent evidence.

Required rule

Unavailable means uncheckable.

If a claim depends on source bytes, a corpus archive, a trace, or a frozen implementation that is unavailable, record the missing artifact and the narrow checks still possible. Do not rebuild a substitute and present it as the committed witness.

Can still review
Public wording, linked source pages, schema shape, logical consistency, and disclosed non-claims.
Cannot conclude
Integrity, exact reproduction, or validity of the missing artifact-dependent derivation.

Submit a challenge

Make disagreement adjudicable.

Include the claim URL or ID, dated software/source snapshot, evidence locators, challenge dimension, strongest alternative explanation, requested disposition, and any source you can share publicly. Describe confidential material first; do not attach restricted source files to an unsolicited email.
