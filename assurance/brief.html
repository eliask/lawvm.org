The institutional problem

A current text hides the transition history that produced it.

Legislation changes through source acts, effective dates, corrigenda, editorial consolidation, multilingual expressions, renumbering, transitional rules, and source-system migrations. A published text may be authoritative, editorial, evidentiary, or merely convenient depending on the jurisdiction. Comparing two final documents rarely explains why they differ.

LawVM treats legal text-state as a derivation. It preserves the source instruction, typed operation, target, temporal coordinate, resulting state, residuals, and comparison disposition so a reviewer can inspect the path instead of trusting a final string.

Project maturity

Beta-stage engineering with bounded assurance.

LawVM is open-source under the MIT license, with substantial working compiler, CLI, API, frontend, test, and evidence surfaces suitable for bounded evaluation. It remains pre-1.0: public interfaces and evidence formats can change, frontend maturity is uneven, and an exact result still depends on its declared software, sources, profile, corpus, and reviewer boundary.

The bounded assurance chain

Seven questions replace one “verified” badge.

exact sources→declared universe→typed operation→admission→observed mutation→temporal state→bounded checking

Source identity

Bind the work to exact manifestations and roots. Identity does not prove source completeness or correctness.

Total accounting

Keep emitted operations, typed rejections, typed observations, and unaccounted effects visible inside the declared universe.

Semantic closure

Require typed target, payload, source anchor, and time. Preserve missing proof instead of guessing.

Mutation account

Compare writer receipts with independently observed before/after paths on wired profiles.

Temporal state

Separate effective time, expiry, temporary overlays, occupancy, identity, branch, and authority regime.

Review boundary

Expose what a separate checker or human reviewer can reconstruct and what still must be trusted.

What a pilot returns

Evidence products for human review

Source and scope manifest

Authorities, manifestations, acquisition dates, hashes, missing sources, work/time boundary, and comparison roles.

Transition account

Typed operations, blocked candidates, recoveries, source pathologies, unexplained residuals, and unchecked units.

Human review queue

Exact source locators, before/after witnesses, confidence limits, alternative explanations, and disposition fields.

Promotion recommendation

Which checks can remain observational, which can block a shadow result, and what evidence is needed for the next claim.

Status discipline

Missing proof and contradiction lead to different decisions.

Evidence already visible

One confirmed case, two public candidate packets

Riigi Teataja reviewed a LawVM-reported omission in Estonia's Auditors Activities Act and corrected the Estonian consolidation. The corresponding English-translation detail remains a project report pending separate adjudication. This one externally adjudicated case supports the case-specific QA claim.

Five additional source/current-text candidates reported from New Zealand and UK work are public with official source links. They remain candidates: publisher notification, acknowledgement, or a later public-text change is not confirmation.

Finland provides a dated replay-construction experiment over a declared corpus. Estonia exercises publisher consistency. The United Kingdom, New Zealand, Norway, Sweden, European Union, and United States exercise other source and effect models. Japan, South Korea, Poland, and Switzerland are staging lanes with explicit source-level claim ceilings and next gates.

Decision and next step

Begin with one source-complete question and a named reviewer.

Define the jurisdiction, institutional role, objective, representative sources, bounded corpus or transition family, and the person who can adjudicate local source authority or drafting practice. LawVM can then propose a shadow evaluation whose strongest possible conclusion is agreed before processing begins.

Confidentiality: do not email confidential or restricted source files. Describe the material and access constraints first so a processing boundary can be agreed.
